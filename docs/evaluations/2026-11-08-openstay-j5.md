# OpenStay's J5: every tree stopped, and the seams that stopped them

**Date:** 2026-11-04 to 2026-11-10 · **Status: closed on what is known.** On 10 November the session's machine was replaced, and the J5 trees and their logs went with it ([what was lost](benchmark/openstay/j5/lost-on-10-november.md)). The ladder's results up to 8 November are read from the logs; what came after is taken from the session's notes and can no longer be checked. The second grading, the reading and the side experiments did not run · **Protocol:** [`benchmark/openstay/`](benchmark/openstay/README.md) · **The kit, written and rehearsed before the runs:** [`benchmark/openstay/j5/`](benchmark/openstay/j5/README.md) · **The rule for a failure:** [`triage.md`](benchmark/openstay/j5/triage.md), written before the failures it judged · **J4:** [its record](2026-11-03-openstay-j4.md)

J5 took the six trees of J4, each from its J4 head, toward requests 51 to 100:
- messaging and its attachments;
- reviews and ratings;
- reports and moderation;
- claims after a stay, and proofs of a direct payment;
- support;
- then internationalisation, taxes and calendar sync.

The ladder is the same as before. Each feature starts with Claude Haiku 5.5, then goes to Sonnet 5.5, then Opus 5.5, and goes one step up only when its attempt fails. A tree stops at the first feature the strongest model also fails, once the triage has decided the failure is the tree's and not the kit's.

## The answer, first

No tree reached a hundred. Every tree stopped on a failure that the triage judged real.

| simple-3 | `0000000000100002000000F` | 51–72 (22) | 73 transfer proof | $20.80 ($2.19 set apart) |

How to read the steps:
- `0`, `1` and `2` are the smallest, the intermediate and the strongest model.
- `F` is the triaged failure.
- Three features were not the attempts' failures, and were credited at the step whose head passes once fixed, by the maintainer's decision, as `triage.md` says. The cost of the steps they forced is set apart.
  - simple-1's 51: the grading's worktree lacked a module (the harness).
  - simple-1's 54: an upload race in the kit's client.
  - simple-3's 71: a field the request did not define.

What the table shows:

- **The simple arm went further: 59 features in all, against 26.**
  - Above the smallest model, the simple arm needed a larger model for 5 of its 59 features: simple-1's 72, simple-2's 59 and 62, simple-3's 61 and 66.
  - The Clean DDD arm needed one for 1 of its 26: clean-1's 62.
- **Both arms stopped on what the requests decided and the code did not do.**
  - Each failure was checked by replaying the test alone.
  - A judge, the intermediate model, read only the request and the test and said whether the text decides it.
  - Where another tree passed the same test, that tree is the proof that it can be passed.

After J5, two follow-on arms ran on corrected kits (Full+ and Simple+), and from 9 November one repair per triaged failure on both arms. No tree reached a hundred there either. The furthest trees reached 85 in each arm, and every correction tried held on its first wording ([below](#one-repair-per-triaged-failure-from-9-november)).

## The hypothesis, after J5

The protocol's hypothesis: *the step features need rises faster in the simple arm than in the Clean DDD arm as the trees grow.*

**Not supported.** The simple trees stepped up more often in J5 than before, five times against none in 150 features, but they kept delivering. The Clean DDD trees stopped sooner. The question the ladder now answers is not which arm needs a larger model, but which one stops, and where.

## Where each tree stopped

The six failures fall on four seams. At each one, a rule the application needed was implicit, and the code had settled the question otherwise before the request arrived.

| Seam | Trees | What the request said | What the code did |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Identity across contexts** (64) | clean-1, clean-3 | The queue lists the reports of a message, a review and a listing, each by the id its maker was answered | Moderation gave each report taken up from Messaging and Reviews an id of its own; `MessageReported` and `ReviewReported` did not carry the report's id. The queue held all four reports, two under ids no client had |
| **Who reaches a command** (51) | clean-2 | A request that needs a signed-in caller and has none is `401` (request 1) | `POST /conversations` answered `202`, and the anonymous caller was refused in the operation, as the architecture's grammar says |
| **A read that changes what is stored** (73) | simple-1, simple-3 | An unpaid booking lapses at the next sweep (requests 30 and 73) | Both trees lapse an overdue booking on any read, a shortcut taken at request 30, so the booking lapsed the moment the test read it |
| **What is left to refund** (66) | simple-2 | Suspending a host refunds what was paid on its stays to come | Steps 0 and 1 failed on a missing payment; step 2 answered `refundable: 0` where `12000` was expected |

How the four seams split between the arms:
- **The Clean DDD trees failed where two contexts meet.** The architecture's own rules, read literally, pointed the wrong way: "an aggregate generates its own identity", "a command is answered 202".
- **The simple trees failed where a shortcut taken early met a rule stated later.** No structure kept a read from writing.

## The kit's failures and the harness's

`triage.md` was written on 6 November, after two failures had been found to be the kit's by hand, after three models had paid for them. Every failure since went through it. What the triage found:

- **simple-1's request 51, a module missing from the grading's worktree** (the harness). Every file failed on a missing `pg`; credited at step 0.
- **Request 54, an upload refused before it was read** (kit). The server answered `413` and closed the connection, and the kit's client read the closed connection as a failure. Corrected with the maintainer's decision; simple-1 credited.
- **Request 71, a field the request did not define** (kit). The request is made to decide it for the trees that had not reached it; simple-3 credited.
- **Full+ clean-1's request 64, delivered by a harness bug.** A grading killed before jest printed a summary named no failing test, and the ladder read the empty list as none. Fixed in the ladder (e708b42): a file that fails without naming a test is a failure. The feature was revoked and run again, and was delivered at step 2. An audit of every delivery of J5, Full+ and the probes found no other case.
- **The machine.**
  - Three container restarts; each ladder was resumed from its log.
  - The kernel killed processes for lack of memory when more than three ladders or four agents ran at once.
  - On the night of 9 November, seven more kills, every one in Full+ clean-1's end-to-end test workers (about 3.5 GB each). Four ladders at once on four CPUs also pushed a grading past its limit. The machine went to three ladders, then to two, one per arm, and no kill was seen with two.
  - On 10 November the machine was replaced, and the trees, the logs and the incident log with it. The J5 trees had never been pushed; the next campaign pushes each tree after every delivery.
  - On 8 November, the ladders were found to run on Node 22, where every tree declares Node 24. One simple tree's own test cannot load an ES module under Node 22 and passes under Node 24 at the same head (rule 3 of the triage).

## Full+: the Clean DDD trees on a kit that says what J5 found

The maintainer kept J5's verdicts and opened a separate arm. Full+ is made of copies of the three Clean DDD trees, each where it stopped, migrated to one kit:
- **Identity is born once.** A thing taken up by another context keeps its id, and the event carries it.
- **A relationship crossed is tested.** A gate step asks each relationship a change crosses for an end-to-end test named for the pair, with a message that says which id to keep.
- **Who reaches a command is said once.** The transport refuses a missing caller at once (`401`), unless the handler is marked `anonymous`.

The kit was rehearsed on one probe per seam before the arm ran:

- **p51, clean-2 at 50:** delivered request 51 at step 0.
- **p64, clean-1 at 63, with the first wording of the gate message** ("the id the upstream answered"): found the message's id instead of the report's at steps 0 and 1, and delivered only at step 2.
- **p64b, the same with the message reworded** ("the id that very request answered; a report of a message is found by the report's id"): delivered at step 0.

| Tree | From | Steps to 8 November | Delivered | Stopped at | Cost to 8 November |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full+ clean-1 | 64 | `2100000000` (64–73) | 64–79 (16) | 80 donations | $38.01 |
| Full+ clean-2 | 51 | `0000000001F` | 51–60 (10) | 61 review rules | $21.38 |
| Full+ clean-3 | 64 | `120100000000` (64–75) | 64–85 (22) | not stopped; last seen at 86 | $32.38 ($66.93 at 85) |

The steps and costs after 8 November were in the lost logs; only the deliveries and the stops are known.

- **Both seams of 64 are passed, by both trees that had stopped there.**
  - Full+ clean-1 needed the strongest model for 64, once the harness bug was fixed.
  - Full+ clean-3 needed the intermediate model.
  - Since then, almost every request has been delivered at step 0, including 73, where every simple tree failed.
- **Full+ clean-2 stopped at 61, on the next seam.** The kit had made `acceptCommand` the one way to ask for a command, which pushed request 60's reply behind a `202`. Request 61 then asked for a refusal named by its rule (`error: "<NAME>"`), and the tree's operations already gave errors another shape. Every model saw the conflict and kept the tree's shape. The verdict stands; the three corrections it shows are kept for the next campaign ([`next-campaign.md`](benchmark/openstay/j5/next-campaign.md)).

- **Full+ clean-1 stopped at 80, as Simple+ simple-3 did, on the same refusal.** Both trees answered `503` for a donation without a provider, a convention each took at request 26; request 1 says a refusal is a `4xx` or an operation that ends in `ERROR`. The triage judged both failures real.
- **Full+ clean-3 went on to 85.** Its step 1 on 85 went unjudged: the grading ran past its 60 minutes under the load of four ladders. The maintainer had that head graded again on a quieter machine. 85's own tests passed; one earlier file failed without naming a test and passed when replayed alone. So 85 counts as delivered at step 1. The tree's agent had raised its own Jest timeout because of the load.

## The CQS probe and Simple+

**The CQS probe.** One sentence was added to the guide of simple-1 and simple-3 at request 72, and 73 was replayed from 72:

> A read never changes what is stored: a `GET` answers from what is there. What changes with time is changed by the background job that runs on its schedule, never by a read.

- **At steps 0 and 1, on both trees, the sentence reached the new code only,** and 73 failed as in J5.
- **At step 2, both trees carried it to the code already written.**
  - simple-3 delivered.
  - simple-1 passed every hidden test up to 73, but left one of its own earlier specs counting on a read that lapses, and failed its own checks.
- **A rule given late is applied to new code; the code it should have shaped is already there.** The sentence goes into the simple arm's starting guide for the next campaign.

**Simple+** continues both trees from there, as a separate arm, to compare with Full+ on the same requests:
- simple-3 from 74, on the probe's delivered head;
- simple-1 from 73 again: a second attempt, counted as such, since its probe failed.

What Simple+ did:
- **simple-3 delivered 74 to 79 and stopped at 80,** on the same `503` as Full+ clean-1.
  - 74 at step 1. Its step 0 was refused by the Node 22 machine fault above, not by its own work; step 1 also rewrote the test so it no longer depends on the Node version.
  - 75 at step 0.
- **simple-1 failed 73 again and stopped.** The probe's sentence said what a read must not do, not that a read still shows what time has changed. The tree dropped a read-time behaviour request 7 decides: a booking reads as expired as soon as its time has passed.
- **simple-2 was not continued** after J5.

So the follow-on arms did not treat the two arms alike. Full+ got three corrections, probed and reworded until they held, on all three trees. Simple+ got one sentence on two trees. The rule below makes the treatment the same.

## One repair per triaged failure (from 9 November)

The maintainer decided on 9 November that every tree of both arms is repaired once after a triaged failure ([the rule and the five corrections](benchmark/openstay/j5/repairs.md)):
- the failure is diagnosed and the earlier request whose rule the tree broke is named;
- a section of the tree's guide states the rule for every feature, never a test's answer;
- the tree replays the request it failed, and the replay is the correction's probe.

| Tree | Replayed | Correction | Replay delivered at | Then | Last seen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| simple-3 | 80 | R2, a refusal | step 0 | 81–84 at step 0; 85 at step 1 | on 86 |
| Full+ clean-1 | 80 | R2, a refusal | step 1 | 81–83 at step 0 | on 84 |
| simple-1 | 73 | R1, time and reads | step 1 | 74–76 at step 0 | at 77, held for a slot |
| Full+ clean-2 | 61 | R3, the API's shapes | not run, no slot | | |
| simple-2 | 66 | R4, what is owed and what was moved | not run, no slot | | |

- **Every correction tried held on its first wording:** R2 on both arms, R1 on the simple arm. R3 and R4 were never tried.
- **clean-1's replay needed the intermediate model only because of the machine.** Its step 0 reached its 90-minute ceiling after the kernel killed its test workers three times for lack of memory.
- **simple-3's 85 needed the intermediate model for a regression.** The smallest model broke 73's read-time test, the one R1 is about; R1 had not reached simple-3, which was past 73 when R1 was written.
- **The night's agents cost $18.68:** clean-1 $6.76, simple-1 $5.96, simple-3 $3.54, clean-3 $2.42.

With one repair each, both arms reached 85. Neither reached a hundred, and two repairs never ran. The failures they repaired were rules a request had decided and the tree had not kept, in both arms; each was fixed by saying the rule once, not by a larger model.

## Two developers on one request

The probe split request 64 by context owner: one agent for Messaging and Reviews, one for Moderation, separate clones, a channel. [Its results](benchmark/openstay/j5/side/two-developers/results.md), two runs per arm:

| Arm | Delivered |
| --- | --- |
| Old kit | 0 of 2 |
| Full+ kit | 0 of 2 |
| Full+ kit with an integrator who runs the gate on the merged `main` and hands each failure to its owner | 2 of 2 |

- The rule made the seam visible in every Full+ run.
- Without the integrator, the request that would have fixed it was sent after the other agent's session had ended, and was lost.

## What changed in the architecture

- **[ADR 33](../adr/0033-identity-is-born-once-and-a-relationship-crossed-is-tested.md):** identity is born once (DOMAIN-IDENTITY), and a relationship crossed is tested. The TypeScript binding turns the gate step on.
- **[RFC 0003](../rfc/0003-who-reaches-a-command.md):** who reaches a command. It changes what every server answers, so it is decided before the code.
- **After a review of these results** (8 November):
  - [ADR 34](../adr/0034-a-relationship-test-must-see-the-change.md): a relationship test's new cases must fail without the change, and the gate runs them at the base;
  - the ladder refuses a Node older than the trees declare, and runs at most three at once ([`machine.mjs`](../../.claude/flow/machine.mjs));
  - [RFC 0004](../rfc/0004-the-integrator-is-a-role-of-the-flow.md): the integrator as a role of the flow;
  - [a draft protocol](benchmark/three-arms/README.md): Simple, Lite and Full at the same time, on requests no kit was tuned on.
- **After request 80 and test 71** (9 November):
  - [ADR 37](../adr/0037-a-decision-for-every-feature-is-taken-once.md): a decision that holds for every feature is taken once, and one taken late is applied to the code already written;
  - the ladder can grade an earlier failing feature on the commit the attempt started from, and its budget counts across resumes;
  - test 71 waits until the unpaid stay reads `lapsed` before asking about it.

## Not done

J5 is closed without these:
- The two follow-on arms to 100, and the repairs of Full+ clean-2 (R3) and simple-2 (R4).
- The second grading, one tree at a time on a quiet machine.
- The independent reading.
- The side experiments not yet run: the limited context, vague requests, the chaos suite, a fresh maintainer, defects to debug, pull requests to review, the fan-out by context, generated boilerplate.

## Biases

- **Three trees per arm, and fewer per seam.** Two trees stopped at 64 and two at 73.
- **The Full+ and Simple+ kits were written after seeing the failures they address,** and are tested on the requests those failures came from. Requests 74 to 100 are the first test out of sample for both.
- **The arms ran under different conditions,** in different hours:
  - the machine: restarts, memory, the Node version found on 8 November;
  - different budgets.
- **The triage's judge is a model, and the maintainer decided every credit.** Every verdict up to 8 November is listed with its evidence in the logs.
- **What happened after 8 November is taken from the session's notes,** written as it ran. The logs that would check it are lost, and the trees cannot be read again.
