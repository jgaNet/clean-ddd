The trees were given under neutral names, as `src/` and `package.json` only, with the tooling's entries removed from the manifest. [`map.txt`](map.txt) says which run each one is: t5 is P3, t6 A run 3, t7 P1, t8 A run 2. The verdicts below are the reader's own. Three of them were checked against the code before being used, and all three hold: t6 N4, t7 N6 and t8 N3.

## What else the reader flagged

- **t6: two sign-ups with one email can both succeed.** The email is checked before an awaited hash, and the save does not check it again. t8 checks at the save, t5 checks again after hashing, and t7 reserves the email before hashing.
- **Login timing reveals whether an email exists in t6, t7 and t8.** An unknown email returns before any hashing. t5 hashes against a decoy salt.
- **Tokens never expire in t5 and t7**, which keep them in an in-memory map. t6 and t8 sign tokens with HMAC and give them a 24-hour expiry.
- **t6 and t8 refuse things the backlog does not name:** archiving an archived note, and restoring a note that is not archived. t6 also refuses switching to the plan already held.
- **t7 answers `GET /notes?tag=Work` with 400.** The API says 200, with the matching notes, here an empty list.
- **t6 and t8 run reactions asynchronously.** The reader suspects a race between scheduling and cancelling a reminder, from reading only, not by running anything. **t6** cancels a reminder at the time the reaction runs, not at the time the note was archived.
- **t6 and t8 might let two concurrent creates both pass the quota at nine notes.** This comes from reading only. In t5 and t7, the check and the insert are synchronous.
- **t6 and t8 keep their state at module level, and they trim titles.** The backlog says nothing about trimming.
