# OpenStay, J0: the framing

The work before the first run, for the maintainer to validate. It follows [the brief](brief.md) and [the protocol](README.md).

It separates three things:

- **decided**: by the maintainer, in this conversation;
- **proposed**: by this framing, with an alternative;
- **open**: the maintainer's to decide.

Nothing is implemented before J1 is validated.

## What the benchmark can and cannot show

**It can show:**

- whether each arm keeps the marketplace's invariants as it grows;
- what each feature costs;
- how often the smallest model is enough, and how often a feature needs a stronger one;
- how far a change spreads;
- whether a tree survives crashes and concurrent writers.

These answer the brief's §9, and its §12 points on coherence, regressions and maintainability.

**It cannot show** that OpenStay works as a service:

- real bookings and real money;
- fees that comply with the law;
- the platform's real running costs;
- moderation, support and operations.

Those need:

- a legal entity;
- a contract with a payment provider;
- legal review: payment services, DSA, DAC7, consumer law, local rules on tourist rentals;
- people to operate it.

No agent run produces any of that. The benchmark yields three codebases per arm and the evidence about them. Which one, if any, becomes the product is a [question](#questions-that-do-not-block-j1) for after J2.

**The payment provider is played by the tests.** A hidden test cannot depend on Stripe's test mode: it needs the network, it is slow, and it does not fail on demand. In the benchmark, the provider is a fake the test runs, at a URL the request names. It acts the way a provider like Stripe Connect does: a payment that needs strong authentication, a capture, a refund, a payout, signed webhooks that arrive late, twice or out of order. The real provider belongs to the product track.

## What this framing changes from the brief

- **No arm sees the brief.**
  - Its §6 sets the architecture by this repository's principles, and lists 22 contexts.
  - Its §10 starts with event storming.

  Given to the simple arm, that is the architecture under another name. Given to the Clean DDD arm only, it is an unmeasured advantage. Each arm gets requests written from §3–5, in product words, one feature at a time.

- **22 contexts are a hypothesis, not a start.** For the pilot, four are enough ([below](#a-candidate-strategic-model-for-the-clean-ddd-arm)). The others arrive when a feature needs them, in a storm, as the maintainer decides. When a context gets split later is one of the things measured.
- **A modular monolith, as the brief's author recommends.** One process per tree, one database, with boundaries enforced in the code. No arm is distributed.
- **The multi-agent arm becomes measurements on chosen wide features**, not a third series ([the protocol](README.md#the-arms)).

## The invariants, as requirements and tests

The brief's §5, said in product words, with the phase that first tests each one:

| # | Invariant | Said to the agents as | First tested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | No platform commission | The quote shows the platform's share, which is zero, and the total is what the host charges plus the identified external fees | Pilot, feature 5 |
| 2 | No markup on payment fees | The fee shown is the provider's fee, to the cent | Phase 2 |
| 3 | No conflicting confirmed bookings | Two travellers booking the same nights at the same moment: exactly one is confirmed, the other is refused with a reason | Pilot, features 6–7 (with concurrent writers) |
| 4 | No financial operation twice on a retry | The same request sent twice with the same key does it once; the provider's webhook delivered twice changes nothing the second time | Pilot (booking), phase 2 (money) |
| 5 | A refund never exceeds what is refundable | Partial refunds add up to at most what was paid, less what was refunded | Pilot (computed), phase 2 (paid) |
| 6 | A payout never exceeds what is due |  | Phase 2 |
| 7 | Cancelled and refunded are distinct | A cancellation states what is refundable; whether it was refunded is a separate state | Pilot, feature 8 |
| 8 | Price and conditions kept as accepted | Changing a listing's price or cancellation policy changes no existing quote or booking | Pilot, features 5, 6, 8 |
| 9 | A donation never changes ranking | The search order is stated, and a donor's listings rank as anyone's | Phase 3 |
| 10 | External fees shown before commitment | Every fee is on the quote, named, before the traveller confirms | Pilot, feature 5 |
| 11 | A direct payment is never shown as verified without proof | A direct payment is "declared by the host", never "verified", unless a proof the platform checked exists | Phase 2 |
| 12 | Asynchronous events can be replayed | A crash at any moment, or a message delivered twice, leaves every booking, notice and payment as if it had happened once | Pilot, feature 10 (and every phase after) |

## A candidate strategic model, for the Clean DDD arm

This is proposed, not decided. It goes to the storm session at J1, where the maintainer decides each boundary as a hot spot. The simple arm never sees it.

**For the pilot, four contexts:**

| Context | Subdomain | Why its boundary falls there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accounts** | Generic | Travellers and hosts are one account with two roles. Organisations and collaborators come with professional hosts, later. |
| **Listings** | Supporting | A listing, its description, capacity, rules and its published state. Search is a read model here, fed with availability by Booking's events. |
| **Booking** | **Core** | The calendar and the bookings in one context, because invariant 3 spans both. The consistency boundary is a listing's calendar. The quote and its snapshot of price and conditions are made here too: the price exists to be booked. |
| **Notifications** | Generic | The notices people are owed, through an outside service that can fail. |

**The challenges to the brief's 22 contexts:**

- **Availability and Booking are one context.** Separating them puts the rule against double booking across a boundary, and it would then need a saga or a lock.
- **Pricing is not a context in the pilot.** Seasonal prices and discounts may make it one later. That is a split to measure.
- **Search is a read model,** not a context with rules.
- **Administration is a set of screens over the others,** not a context.
- **Payment orchestration, refunds, payouts and the payment ledger are one context in phase 2,** with several aggregates. Direct payments may be a second context, since their guarantees differ.
- **Identity, Authorization, Organizations and Hosts start as one context,** Accounts. When professional hosts arrive, whether it splits is decided by a storm.

**Domain events of the pilot, to be named in the storms:**

- an account was registered;
- a listing was published, suspended, archived;
- dates were blocked;
- a quote was issued;
- a booking was requested, confirmed, declined, expired, cancelled;
- a notice was sent.

## The pilot: ten features

Phase 1 of the brief, without payment. The requests will be written in product words at J1, each with its API. The hidden tests are written at J1 too.

| # | Feature | What its hidden tests do | Invariants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Accounts**: sign up as a traveller, a host or both; sign in | Sign up, sign in, a wrong password refused, an email used twice refused | — |
| 2 | **Publish a listing**: a draft with title, city, capacity, nightly price and cleaning fee; publish, suspend, archive | Only its host changes it; a suspended listing is not bookable | — |
| 3 | **Search**: by city, dates and number of guests, in a stated order | A listing with a booking or blocked dates on those nights is absent; capacity respected; order as stated | 9 (the order is stated) |
| 4 | **The host's calendar**: block and free dates; minimum and maximum nights | Blocked nights cannot be booked; stays outside the limits are refused | 3 |
| 5 | **A quote**: the total for given dates, item by item, valid for a while | Nightly prices, cleaning fee, external fees named, platform share zero; a price changed afterwards leaves the quote as it was | 1, 8, 10 |
| 6 | **Book instantly** from a quote | Twenty travellers book the same nights at once, in one process and in two processes on one database: exactly one is confirmed. The same request sent twice with one key gives one booking. | 3, 4, 8 |
| 7 | **Request to book**: the host accepts or declines; the request expires | A request holds the nights until it is decided or expires; an expired request frees them; accepting after expiry is refused | 3 |
| 8 | **Cancel a booking** under the cancellation policy it was booked with | The refundable amount follows the policy as accepted, even if the host has changed it since; cancelled is not refunded | 5, 7, 8 |
| 9 | **A booking's history**: every transition, who made it, when; the guest's contact shown to the host only once confirmed | Transitions in order; no contact before confirmation | — |
| 10 | **Notify the host** of each new booking or request, through an outside service | The service refuses three times, then accepts. A server killed at any moment, then restarted, still sends every notice exactly once. | 12 |

**What the tests need from the API:**

- `DATABASE_PATH`, so a test can restart the server on the same data;
- `NOTIFY_URL`, the outside service the test plays;
- `BOOKING_REQUEST_TTL_SECONDS`, so expiry can be tested without waiting a day.

The requests name all three, as the robustness kit names its own.

## Decisions for the maintainer

Decided in this conversation:

- the checkpoints J0–J5;
- the smallest model first, escalated one step at a time (intermediate, then strongest), per tree and per feature, under a rule written in advance;
- everything about each attempt recorded.

**Decided by the maintainer on 2026-10-31, before J1:**

- **Decisions 1–9 and 11 as proposed.** The pilot's ceiling is **$200, firm**.
- **Decision 10 is moot.** The notes robustness campaign ran the same day ([the record](../../2026-10-31-flow-robustness.md)).
- **The Clean DDD trees start from the corrected reference.** That reference gives the startup relay a unit of work (ADR 24's revision), so `create` gives each tree the fix the robustness campaign found missing.
- **A command answers in either protocol, and the hidden tests accept both.**
  - Synchronously: `2xx` with its result, or `4xx` when it is refused.
  - Asynchronously: `202 { operationId }`, followed until the operation ends in `SUCCESS` or `ERROR`.

  The notes campaigns imposed the second protocol on every arm, a bias [the control](../control/README.md#biases-written-down-now) names. Here each arm keeps the protocol its design gives it. The tests check whether a command was accepted or refused, and the state it left, never the name of a refusal.

| # | Decision | Proposed | Alternative | What decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The arms | Simple and Clean DDD, three trees each; the fan-out measured on chosen wide features | Three arms for the whole series | The fan-out pays only on wide features (phase 4 records), so a whole series of it would buy little and cost about 1.7 times more |
| 2 | The models | Three models of one family, versions pinned per series: the smallest first, then the intermediate, then the strongest, one step per failure; a strongest-model witness at the pilot | Straight from the smallest to the strongest | The intermediate step costs less than the strongest, and the step a feature needs measures more finely than a yes or no |
| 3 | The stack | TypeScript, Node, Fastify, SQLite | Python | The Clean DDD arm needs its full tooling (gate, storms, `--fix`), which only TypeScript has; the harness and the grader are TypeScript |
| 4 | Where the kit lives | Here, in `docs/evaluations/benchmark/openstay/`, as every kit so far; the trees outside, in their own repositories | A separate repository from the start | The kit and its tests are never in a tree an agent works in, either way. A product repository is a product-track question |
| 5 | The payment provider | Played by the tests in the benchmark; Stripe's test mode in the product track | Stripe's test mode in the hidden tests | A hidden test must fail on demand and run offline |
| 6 | Money | One currency (euro), amounts as integers in cents | Several currencies from the start | Several currencies are a change of phase 4, and more useful unannounced |
| 7 | The pilot's budget | A firm ceiling of **$200**: $60–130 for the series, $30–60 for the witness, the rest held back | Without a ceiling | **The maintainer's figure** |
| 8 | Who writes the tests | This framing's author, reviewed by a fresh agent that has only the requests, then rehearsed with `dry-run` | A fresh agent writes them | Writing them costs more and finds fewer of its own defects than reviewing does |
| 9 | The storms | Decided with the maintainer before each feature's run, as in phase 4, with their time counted as the Clean DDD arm's cost | No storms | Without storms the arm is not this repository's method |
| 10 | The notes robustness campaign | Postponed again: the pilot tests the same guarantees (features 6 and 10) on trees built for them | Run it first ($40–60) | It answers a narrower question, on trees grown without those requirements |
| 11 | The pilot's ten features | [As listed](#the-pilot-ten-features) | Another cut of phase 1 | Each of the brief's pilot-testable invariants is tested at least once |

## Risks

| Risk | Kind | What reduces it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A kit defect is paid for in runs | Benchmark | Every kit rehearsed with `dry-run`; a fresh agent reviews the tests; amendments recorded |
| The smallest model fails nearly everything | Benchmark | The escalation rule; the pilot shows it within ten features, and decision 2 can be revisited by starting at the intermediate model |
| The cost per feature grows faster than estimated | Financial | A ceiling per checkpoint, reserved before agents start; a re-estimate at every checkpoint |
| A pinned model is withdrawn mid-series | Benchmark | A witness series on both models before continuing |
| One author for the method, the requests and the tests | Bias | The review by a fresh agent; hot spots decided by the maintainer; the biases on record |
| The requests leak the architecture | Bias | Written from the brief's §3–5 only; the review checks for architecture words |
| Concurrency on SQLite is not a server database's | Technical | Two processes on one file from the pilot; the storage migration at J4 |
| Chaos tests are flaky | Technical | Seeded delays; each chaos test graded twice, a failure in either counts |
| The brief's production ambitions outrun the benchmark | Scope | [Stated](#what-the-benchmark-can-and-cannot-show); left to the product track and to people |
| Fees, payments and rental rules are misread | Regulatory | The benchmark encodes stated rules only, never a legal reading; a legal reading belongs to the product track, by a lawyer |

## Questions that do not block J1

- Which legal entity runs OpenStay, and in which country first.
- Which payment provider: Stripe Connect is one candidate. The rules on holding funds decide which flows are possible.
- Which tree, if any, becomes the product, or whether the product starts again from what the benchmark learned.
- Who moderates and supports, and with what budget, under the brief's §2.4.
- Whether the conversational interface is in the first product release.
